## 1. Findings

1. **Different theorems, not two proofs of one.** TB states *unforced* breakdown (`f ≡ 0`, compactly supported or periodic datum) **conditionally** on a self-similar profile or a certificate; OA states Clay alternatives (C) and (D) **unconditionally**, with witness `u₀ ≡ 0` and a nonzero smooth force, plus unforced Euler blow-up. TB's conclusions would imply (C), (D) *and* refute (A), (B); OA's (C), (D) say nothing about (A), (B) (§5).
2. **Time derivative.** TB uses `deriv` at `t > 0`; FC/OA-comparator uses `derivWithin … (Set.Ici 0)` at `t ≥ 0`; OA's internal layer uses `fderiv … 1` at interior times. All three are evaluated only where joint `ContDiffOn ℝ ∞` on the closed half-space makes them genuine derivatives. No junk value (`deriv = 0` at a non-differentiable point) is reachable inside any solution class (§3).
3. **Torus and pressure.** Neither statement layer uses `AddCircle` or `UnitAddTorus`: the torus is a unit-periodic lift to `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)`. TB's torus class does **not** require periodic pressure; this enlarges the class being excluded (a stronger claim) and the library closes the gap with a proved Galilean reduction. FC and OA require periodic pressure (Clay erratum); with a nonzero force that clause is not removable by the same argument (§4).
4. **Integrals.** FC's energy bound is a Bochner integral guarded by `MemLp`; TB uses `∫⁻` and needs no guard. TB's `fractionalPowerAt` is a Bochner integral whose normalizer diverges for `α ≤ 1`, so the operator silently becomes `0` there; the theorem hypothesis `1 < α` is what keeps it meaningful (§3.3, §6.3).
5. **Certificate interface.** TB's `CellSystemCondition` is a `Prop` over real-valued data. The repository contains no inhabitant, no data, and no interval-arithmetic layer; the 128-bit Arb/FLINT verification is outside Lean, and the published text carries a patch that upstream says was not rebuilt (§6).
6. **Non-vacuity.** No bundled hypothesis is degenerate in the checked parameters (`ν`, periods, parameter sets, nonzero profile). OA's datum is degenerate by design (`u₀ ≡ 0`), which (C)/(D) permit. Whether TB's hypotheses are *satisfiable* cannot be decided from the repository (§7).

**3.2 Junk values are unreachable.** Mathlib totalizes derivatives: `deriv f t = 0` and `fderiv f x = 0` at points of non-differentiability, and `derivWithin f s t = 0` where `f` is not differentiable within `s` or `s` lacks unique derivatives at `t`. In each class above the smoothness field rules these cases out:

- `t > 0` is interior to `Ici 0 ×ˢ univ`, so `ContDiffOn` gives `ContDiffAt` and the time slice is differentiable. OA proves this explicitly as `differentiable_time_slice` [`NavierStokes/ComparatorBridge.lean#L183-L189`](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler/blob/f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd/NavierStokes/ComparatorBridge.lean#L183-L189); the same argument applies to TB's class.
- At `t = 0`, `Set.Ici 0` has unique derivatives and `v x ·` is `ContDiffOn` on it, so FC's `derivWithin` is the true right derivative.
- Spatial operators act on slices `v · t` with `t ≥ 0`, which are globally `ContDiff ℝ ∞` (OA `smooth_space_slice` [`NavierStokes/ComparatorBridge.lean#L176-L181`](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler/blob/f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd/NavierStokes/ComparatorBridge.lean#L176-L181); TB `contDiff_slice` [`Final.lean#L13-L15`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Breakdown/Torus/Final.lean#L13-L15)).
- Values of `u` at `t < 0` are unconstrained in every class and never enter: `deriv` at `t > 0` is local, and `derivWithin … (Ici 0)` ignores the left side.

FC documents the junk behavior of its divergence and proves the corresponding lemma [`ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean#L77-L90`](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler/blob/f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd/ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean#L77-L90). ## 4. Space, periodicity, pressure

**4.1 No quotient torus in any statement.** `AddCircle`, `UnitAddCircle` and `UnitAddTorus` occur only inside the libraries (TB: 45 files under `Breakdown/`; OA: 115 files, 108 of them under `Euler/`) and in none of the statement or comparator files. Periodicity is always invariance of a function on `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)` under the three unit shifts, quantified at every point, which also gives the negative shifts.

**4.2 Pressure periodicity.**

- TB `BreakdownOnTorus` excludes every smooth `(u, p)` with `u` unit-periodic for `t ≥ 0`; `p` is only required to be jointly smooth [`Challenge.lean#L41-L45`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Challenge.lean#L41-L45). The README states this as "whatever the pressure" [`lean/README.md#L35-L39`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/README.md#L35-L39).
- FC requires `IsOnePeriodic (p · t)` for `t ≥ 0` and attributes it to the errata appended to the Clay statement [`ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean#L258-L269`](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler/blob/f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd/ComparatorChallenges/NavierStokes.lean#L258-L269); the upstream module docstring says the same [`formal-conjectures NavierStokes.lean#L49-L53`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/8bf45ed70d48b2b2a501de9c00b26bfa38c573ee/FormalConjectures/Millenium/NavierStokes.lean#L49-L53).
- TB's larger class is handled in the library, not assumed away: `MovingFrameReduction` says a smooth solution with periodic velocity can be replaced by one with the same datum and periodic pressure [`StatementsSmooth.lean#L38-L42`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Breakdown/Torus/StatementsSmooth.lean#L38-L42), it is proved as `movingFrameReduction` [`MovingFrame.lean#L175`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Breakdown/Torus/MovingFrame.lean#L175), and it is consumed by the final torus theorem [`Reduction.lean#L23-L25`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Breakdown/Torus/Reduction.lean#L23-L25). The key step shows `p t (x + eᵢ) − p t x` is independent of `x` [`MovingFrame.lean#L80-L83`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/Breakdown/Torus/MovingFrame.lean#L80-L83), i.e. `p = a(t)·x + periodic`. - *(paper argument)* This reduction depends on `f ≡ 0`. With a force, a solution whose pressure is `a(t)·x + periodic` becomes, after the boost, a periodic-pressure solution for the *translated* force `f(t, x + X(t))`. OA's (D) therefore excludes exactly the Clay-erratum class and makes no claim about competitors with non-periodic pressure. **6.3 The fractional operator** *(paper argument on the definitions)*. `fractionalPowerAt α f x` is `c_α⁻¹ ∫ ‖z‖^(−(3+2α)) · (f(x+z) − f(x) − Df(x)z − ½D²f(x)[z,z]) dz` as a Bochner integral, with `c_α = ∫ ‖z‖^(−(3+2α)) (cos z₀ − 1 + z₀²/2) dz` [`FractionalChallenge.lean#L23-L30`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/FractionalChallenge.lean#L23-L30).

- `c_α` is finite and positive exactly for `1 < α < 2` (integrand `~ r^(−1−2α)` at infinity, `~ r^(1−2α)` at the origin). For `α ≤ 1` the integrand of `c_α` is not integrable, so the Bochner integral is `0`, Lean's `0⁻¹ = 0`, and the operator is identically `0`: `IsFractionalSolution` would then be the Euler system under another name. The binder `1 < α` in both theorems [`FractionalChallenge.lean#L74-L80`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/FractionalChallenge.lean#L74-L80) excludes this.
- For smooth `f` the pointwise integrand is absolutely integrable at the origin when `α < 3/2` (the remainder is `O(‖z‖³)`) and at infinity when `α > 1` and `f` is bounded or in `L²`, so on `(1, 5/4)` the definition is the genuine `(−Δ)^α` with symbol `+‖ξ‖^(2α)`, and the sign in the momentum equation is dissipative [`FractionalChallenge.lean#L37-L39`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/FractionalChallenge.lean#L37-L39). Finite energy on `ℝ³` or periodicity on the torus makes the integrand integrable for every member of the solution classes.
- The profile decay exponent `2α − 1` is below `3/2` exactly when `α < 5/4`: the hypothesis range is the energy-supercritical range, where a scale-invariant profile is not in `L²`. This variant is not used by the paper [`lean/README.md#L20-L21`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/README.md#L20-L21).

**Satisfiability of TB's hypotheses.** The checks above show no hypothesis is trivially false or trivially true in the parameters examined; they do not show any hypothesis is satisfiable. Two remarks *(paper argument, literature as we read it)*:

- A profile constant in `s` cannot satisfy the smooth hypothesis: the decay bound puts `W` in `L^q` for every `q > 3`, and Tsai's theorem (1998) excludes nonzero Leray self-similar profiles there. Any inhabitant must be genuinely time-periodic in `s` (discretely self-similar up to the isometry `R`). The decay `(1+‖y‖)^(−1)` is the weak-`L³` borderline, outside the `L^∞_t L³_x` regularity theorem of Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák, so the hypothesis is not excluded by those results.
- Junk values on the hypothesis side can only weaken a hypothesis (the implication is kernel-checked either way), so they bear on fidelity to the paper, not on soundness. The `.toReal` and Bochner sites inspected in the two certificate records are guarded by finiteness fields of `IsPerturbation` and `IsProfileField` [`CertificateChallenge.lean#L40-L62`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/CertificateChallenge.lean#L40-L62), [`CertificateChallenge.lean#L79-L88`](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/blob/ad53419de079eb8f95f60313473374624a348546/lean/CertificateChallenge.lean#L79-L88).
